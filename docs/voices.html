Voice samples

Hear how each Retroreader speech engine approaches the same text at a comfortable demonstration rate.

The numerical rate scales differ between engines, so the values are not directly comparable. Approximate words per minute are calculated from the complete recording, including pauses, and are intended only as a practical comparison. These samples use the same synthesis code and voice data as their corresponding firmware builds. Dates identify the earliest relevant public lineage; where the sample uses a later build, both dates are shown.

OpenEVV

1999 lineage. OpenEVV reconstructs IBM's Embedded ViaVoice text-to-speech engine from 1999 Windows objects: technology that screen-reader users subsequently came to know through Eloquence. Retroreader supports rates from 60 to 140; the maximum produces approximately 750 words per minute on the sample text.

Retroreader rate 80; approximately 255 words per minute.

Pico

2009. Pico is the small-footprint SVOX speech synthesizer released through the Android Open Source Project and included with Android's early text-to-speech support. Retroreader supports rates from 20 to 500; the maximum produces approximately 460 words per minute on the sample text.

Retroreader rate 100; approximately 135 words per minute.

Retroreader rate 100; approximately 135 words per minute.

DECtalk

DTC-01

1983. The DTC-01 was Digital Equipment Corporation's original DECtalk hardware speech synthesizer. Its first owner's manual appeared in December 1983, before its commonly cited 1984 commercial release. Sample rate 180; approximately 125 words per minute. Retroreader allows rates up to 350 words per minute.

DECtalk 4.3

1990s DECtalk software lineage. This build brings the classic DECtalk 4.3 software synthesizer to Retroreader, with its nine familiar voices and fast formant speech. Sample rate 180; approximately 135 words per minute. Retroreader allows rates up to 600 words per minute.

OpenBST 1998 — Keynote GOLD

1995 lineage; 1998 sampled build. OpenBST recreates Berkeley Speech Technologies' BeSTspeech synthesizer, also known from HumanWare's Keynote GOLD. The preserved family includes a 1995 English generation and the 1998 English build sampled here. Retroreader rate 100; approximately 110 words per minute.

Monologue — First Byte ProVoice

1980s lineage; 1997 engine. Monologue uses First Byte's concatenative ProVoice technology and the compact XEN11K8 English voice. Sample settings: Retroreader rate 130, pitch 50, voice volume 85% and a 100 millisecond sentence pause; approximately 235 words per minute.

TruVoice

1998. Lernout & Hauspie's compact TruVoice synthesizer provides ten character voices through its US English engine. This sample uses the default Peter voice at Retroreader rate 175, producing approximately 175 words per minute. Retroreader allows rates up to approximately 250 words per minute.

DoubleTalk Hybrid — experimental

Early-1990s hardware lineage. DoubleTalk Hybrid is based on the RC Systems DoubleTalk PC, an 8-bit ISA speech card built around an Intel 80C188EB processor and an 8-bit DAC. Retroreader retains the original firmware front end for pronunciation and prosody while rendering its acoustic instructions as native ESP32 code. This experimental approach preserves the familiar voice while making it fast enough for portable reading.

The sample uses Retroreader rate 120 with rate boost off and produces approximately 220 words per minute. Rate 140 produces approximately 290 words per minute with these settings; enabling rate boost at that maximum raises the sample to approximately 400 words per minute. Actual speed varies with the text and articulation setting.

Dolphin Apollo II

Early-1990s British hardware lineage. Apollo II was a popular external speech synthesizer from Dolphin Systems. This reconstruction ports the original UK firmware to native C and replaces the lost NEC speech-DSP program with a newly written 10 kHz Holmes-style formant renderer. The sample uses Apollo speed 4 and produces approximately 182 words per minute.

Speech Plus Prose 2000

1988 hardware firmware. This build reconstructs the complete Prose 2000 v3.4.1 text, pronunciation, prosody and formant-synthesis pipeline in portable C. The sample uses engine rate 180 and the default voice, producing approximately 166 words per minute. Three voice characters are available in Retroreader.

RSynth

1994. RSynth is a compact Klatt-formant speech synthesizer. This sample uses Retroreader rate 160 and produces approximately 172 words per minute.

Voice provenance and source projects
OpenEVV
A portable reconstruction of IBM's 1999 Embedded ViaVoice engine by Stanislaw Przedzinkowski. Retroreader's port is based on Mudb0y/openevv.
Pico
The SVOX Pico engine and voices released through the Android Open Source Project. Retroreader uses Igor Huguet's portable picotts project.
DECtalk DTC-01
A reconstruction of Digital Equipment Corporation's original DTC-01 hardware voice. Retroreader uses DECtalk DTC-01 Reborn by rommix0.
DECtalk 4.3
Digital Equipment Corporation's software DECtalk 4.3 lineage, preserved by the community DECtalk project.
OpenBST — Keynote GOLD
A portable reconstruction of Berkeley Speech Technologies' BeSTspeech, familiar from HumanWare's Keynote GOLD. Retroreader uses Mudb0y/openbst.
Monologue
First Byte's ProVoice technology with the XEN11K8 English voice. Retroreader's port is based on rommix0's First Byte ProVoice Reborn.
TruVoice
Centigram's TruVoice, later associated with Lernout & Hauspie. Retroreader's port is based on rommix0's TruVoice reconstruction.
DoubleTalk Hybrid
RC Systems' DoubleTalk PC firmware front end, combined with Retroreader's native ESP32 acoustic renderer. Its board and 80C188 emulation derive from David Sexton's DoubleTalk PC emulator.
Dolphin Apollo II
Dolphin Systems' UK Apollo II hardware firmware, reconstructed as native C by rommix0 in Dolphin Apollo II Reborn. Its firmware output is verified byte-for-byte against the original ROM. Because the original NEC DSP ROM has been lost, the project supplies a newly written Holmes-style formant renderer rather than distributing or emulating that DSP program.
Speech Plus Prose 2000
Speech Plus and Telesensory Systems' Prose 2000 hardware firmware version 3.4.1 from 1988. Retroreader uses the portable C reconstruction in Prose 2000 Reborn, including its reconstructed NEC uPD7720 DSP program.
RSynth
A Klatt-formant speech synthesizer originally developed by Nick Ing-Simmons from 1994 onward. Retroreader uses the maintained native C version from the RSynth NVDA project, adapted to stream 16 kHz PCM on the ESP32-S3. RSynth is distributed under the GNU LGPL 2.1 or later.
