Pronunciation substitutions

Retroreader replaces text immediately before it reaches the selected speech engine. Files are UTF-8, tab-separated text: text to find in column one and its replacement in column two. Do not use spaces in place of the tab.

NVDA    N V D A
Dr. doctor

Put files in .rr. substitutions.tsv applies to every voice. An optional engine file loads afterwards and overrides a global entry with the same first column. The combined limit is 160 entries.

Plain replacements belong in the global file. Phonemes and commands belong only in the matching engine file. Restart Retroreader after editing one.

Escapes for control-character engines

Replacement fields recognise \\e for Escape, \\xNN for a non-zero hex byte, \\r, \\n, \\t, and \\\\ for a literal backslash. They are decoded only in column two. Thus \\x01 produces DoubleTalk's Control-A and \\e[2I produces Prose's phoneme-input command.

OpenEVV: IBM Speech Pronunciation Rules

OpenEVV accepts IBM ViaVoice/Eloquence annotations and Speech Pronunciation Rules (SPR). Enclose an SPR pronunciation in backquote-square brackets:

though  `[.1Do]
shocking    `[.1Sa.0kIG]
construction    `[kXn1strHkSXn]

SPR is case-sensitive. 1 is primary stress, 2 secondary stress, 0 unstressed, and . is a syllable boundary (optional in English).

DECtalk DTC-01

DTC-01's square-bracket phonemic mode is enabled by the engine. Put phonemes directly in brackets, for example [d'iyk]. Its two-character alphabet is case-insensitive.

Set Symbols
Vowels aa ae ah ao aw ax ay eh ey ih ix iy ow oy uh uw yu
R-coloured vowels ir er ar or ur rr
Syllabics el em en
Consonants b ch d dh f g hx jh k l m n nx p r s sh t th v w y z zh
Silence/allophones _ rx lx dx tx q
Modifier Meaning
', backquote, " Primary, secondary and emphatic stress; before vowel
-, *, #, space Syllable, morpheme, compound and word boundaries
, . ? ! Clause endings
phoneme<duration,pitch> Duration in ms and ending pitch in Hz
pitch 1 to 37 Musical C2 through C5 with vibrato

Stops p t k b d g cannot be sustained. Other bracket commands include :ra 120 to :ra 350, :cp and :pp from 0 to 9000 ms, voices :np :nb :nh :nf :nk :nr :nu :nv, and :dv voice-design parameters. DTC-01 does not use the newer [:phoneme arpabet ...] command.

DECtalk 4.3

When substitutions are loaded, Retroreader automatically prefixes every DECtalk 4.3 utterance with [:phoneme arpabet speak on]. A replacement can therefore contain bracketed ARPAbet directly.

Set Symbols
Vowels e a i E A I O o u ^ W Y R c @ U | x
Consonants p t k f T s S C w y h l L N b d g v D z Z J m n G r q Q & F
R-coloured vowels B K P M j
Stress/boundaries ' primary, backquote secondary, " emphatic, # ( ) * ~ -

Commands include [:rate 200], [:name paul], [:volume 50], [:comma 150], [:period 300], [:punct ...], [:pronounce ...], [:say ...], [:dv ...], and [:tone ...]. Avoid index, sync, play, logging and error commands because Retroreader owns buffering and navigation.

DoubleTalk Hybrid

Enter direct phoneme mode with \\x01D, separate every phoneme with a space, and return to text mode with \\x01T.

computer    \x01DK AX M P YY UW DX ER\x01T
Set Symbols
English vowels AA AE AH AW AX AY EH ER EY IH IX IY OW OY UH UW
English consonants B CH D DH DX F G H J K KX L M N NG P PX R S SH T TH TX V W WH Y YY Z ZH
Additional multilingual sounds A E EI EW I NY O RR U
Pauses apostrophe short, space variable, comma medium, period long

Inside phoneme mode, nn sets pitch 0–99, / and backslash raise and lower pitch temporarily, + and - alter speed, and > and < alter volume. Ordinary commands also use \\x01, for example \\x019S for speed 9. Always return to text mode in the same replacement.

Pico

Pico accepts one-word X-SAMPA in its native compact tag:

word    <phoneme ph='"w3:d'/>

The inventory is language-dependent. The useful English core is:

Set X-SAMPA symbols
Stops/fricatives/nasals p b t d k g f v T D s z S Z h m n N
Other consonants t_S d_Z l r\\ w j
US/UK vowels I E { V Q U @ 3 A i: u: O:
Diphthongs e_I a_I O_I a_U @U I@ E@ U@
Modifiers " primary stress, % secondary, . syllable, : length, = syllabic

Use the symbols of the installed Pico language. The compact tag, rather than a full <speak> document, is what reaches Pico's low-level engine.

TruVoice

In normal text mode, prefix each TruVoice phoneme symbol with &.

Sound group Symbols
Vowels/diphthongs A a b c E e f g I i O o U u v w y 3 4 5 @ |
Consonants B C D d F G H J K L l M N n P q R S s T t V W X x Y Z z ~
Stress 1 primary, 2 secondary

Examples: &C “ch”, &s “sh”, &X unvoiced “th”, &x voiced “th”, &~ “ng”, and &@ schwa. Symbols are case-sensitive. Advanced users may use \\e[1I for continuous phoneme mode and \\e[0I to return to text, but ampersand phonemes are safer in a single replacement.

Prose 2000

Prose 2000 1988 supports direct phoneme input. Use \\e[2I, the engine's two-character phoneme names, then \\e[0I to return to text. Primary and secondary stress are 1 and 2 after the vowel; " is emphatic stress. A phoneme may be followed by !duration;pitch (or /duration;pitch) to override timing and pitch. Keep the mode switch and reset in one replacement.

The 1983 Prose variant lacks the same documented phoneme-spelling front end; use plain text substitutions there.

Dolphin Apollo II

The Apollo port retains the original firmware's command parser. For a mixed text-and-phoneme replacement, prefix every phoneme with an underscore. This is safer than switching the entire input stream into phonetic mode.

cat _K_AE_T
again   _A_G_P_E_N
Sound group Phonemes
Vowels/diphthongs _AA _AI _AR _AW _A _EE _EI _ER _E _IA _IE _I _OA _00 _OR _OW _OY _O _UU _U
Stops _B _D _G _K _KK _P _PP _T _TT
Fricatives/affricates _CH _DH _F _H _J _SH _S _TH _V _X _ZH _Z
Nasals/liquids/glides _L _M _N _NG _Q _QQ _R _W _Y

_QQ is a slight pause. _KK, _PP and _TT are the variants heard in words such as “school”, “speech” and “steel”. The manual's _00 uses two zeroes rather than two letter O characters, as in “good”. Use @X1 for an entirely phonetic run and @X0 to return to ordinary text; both switches must be in the same replacement.

Apollo also exposes its voice-shaping commands in replacements:

Command Effect
@W1–@W9 Speed, approximately 80–580 words per minute
@F0–@FF, @F+, @F- Pitch centre or a small pitch step
@R0–@R7 Overall prosody/excitability
@Q0–@Q9 Inter-word pause
@D0–@DF End-of-phrase pause
@B1–@B8 Voicing, from breathy whisper to fully voiced
@M00–@M3F Mark–space ratio, affecting voice depth
@K0, @K1 Male or non-male formant table
@$0–@$7 Source/filter combination
@V1–@V6 Original preset voices; also resets custom formant offsets
@A0–@AF Synthesis volume within an utterance

The source/filter values are: 0 male/default, 1 non-male/default, 2 male/spike, 3 non-male/spike, 4 male/cut-down, 5 non-male/cut-down, 6 male/reduced-high, and 7 non-male/reduced-high.

The advanced formant command is @Udhh+ or @Udhh-, where d selects the parameter and hh is a hexadecimal offset. Parameters are 0 nasal-formant frequency, 1–3 formant frequencies F1–F3, 4 low-frequency amplitude, 5–8 amplitudes A1–A4, and 9 voicing. The fourth formant frequency is fixed.

These offsets persist in the Apollo firmware state. Undo an experimental offset with the same value and opposite sign, or select a preset with @V1 through @V6 to clear the offsets. Retroreader reapplies its ordinary rate, pitch, expression and volume at the start of each utterance, but it does not automatically erase formant offsets inserted by a substitution.

Apollo also accepts @Rchh to alter one of its internal prosody bytes (c is A through Z, hh is hexadecimal). Those byte meanings are not named in the user manual, so this low-level form is intentionally documented as experimental rather than presented as 26 reliable user controls.

Engines currently limited to plain replacements

The current Monologue and OpenBST adapters do not expose a validated direct-phoneme syntax. Their engine-specific files still support ordinary pronunciation spellings. This is an adapter limitation, not a claim that the original products contain no lower-level phoneme data.
